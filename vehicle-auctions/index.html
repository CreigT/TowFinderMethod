How Public Vehicle Auctions Work

Cities, counties, police departments, fleet agencies, and contracted auction providers may sell surplus, retired, seized, or impounded vehicles.

Where do public vehicle auctions come from?

Vehicle auctions may be organized by counties, cities, law-enforcement agencies, government fleet departments, or contracted auction companies. The official source should identify who is conducting the sale and the terms that apply.

Can anyone bid?

Some auctions are open to the general public, while others require registration, deposits, business licenses, bidder numbers, or other eligibility requirements. Always read the official auction rules before bidding.

Are auction vehicles cheaper?

They can be, but price alone does not determine value. Many auction vehicles are sold as-is and may have mechanical, cosmetic, title, registration, towing, storage, or repair issues. Buyers should consider the full cost before bidding.

What should I verify before bidding?

Check the auction date and location, bidder requirements, payment rules, buyer fees, title status, vehicle inspection rules, pickup deadlines, taxes, towing requirements, and whether the vehicle is sold as-is.

How do I find official auctions?
