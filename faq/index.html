Towed Vehicle & Auction FAQ

Clear answers to common questions people ask after a vehicle is towed or when they are looking for a public vehicle auction.

My car got towed. How do I find it?

Start with the city or jurisdiction where the vehicle was parked or last seen. Gather your license plate and VIN if available, then use the applicable local towing, police, parking, or impound resource. TowFinder Method helps route you toward the appropriate local path.

How can I find out which towing company has my car?

The towing company may depend on the city, police agency, parking authority, property owner, or private tow operator involved. Start with the exact location where the vehicle was removed and check the official local resource.

Can I find my towed vehicle using my license plate?

Some jurisdictions and towing systems allow searches using a license plate, VIN, or other vehicle information. Availability varies by location.

What should I do immediately after discovering my car was towed?

Confirm the vehicle was not stolen, identify the city or jurisdiction where it was parked, gather your license plate and VIN if available, and contact the applicable towing or local-government resource.

How much does it cost to get a towed vehicle back?

Costs vary by location and can include towing charges, storage fees, administrative fees, and other authorized charges. Storage costs may increase the longer the vehicle remains impounded.

What documents do I need to retrieve a towed vehicle?

Requirements vary, but commonly requested items include government-issued identification, proof of ownership or registration, proof of insurance, and payment for applicable fees.

Where can I find public vehicle auctions?

Government agencies, counties, cities, police departments, fleet departments, and contracted auction companies may sell surplus, impounded, or retired vehicles. Always verify the event through the official agency or auction provider.

Can anyone buy a vehicle at a county auction?

Some public auctions are open to everyone, while others may require registration, deposits, licenses, or other eligibility requirements. Read the official auction rules before bidding.
