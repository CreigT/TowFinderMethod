Car Got Towed? Here’s What To Do Next.

The fastest path is to identify the jurisdiction first, then use the correct official towing, police, parking, or impound resource.

Confirm where the vehicle was last parked

Write down the city, street, parking lot, apartment complex, business, or other location where the vehicle was last seen. The correct tow resource often depends on the exact jurisdiction.

Gather your vehicle information

Have your license plate, VIN if available, make, model, color, and approximate time the vehicle disappeared. Some local systems use one or more of these details.

Determine whether the tow was public or private

A police or parking-related tow may route through a city or law-enforcement process. A vehicle removed from private property may involve a private towing company. The starting point can be different.

Use the official local source

Check the applicable police department, parking authority, city towing resource, impound system, or officially listed towing provider. Avoid relying on unverified third-party information when money, release requirements, or legal status are involved.

Ask what is required for release

Requirements vary, but may include government-issued identification, registration or proof of ownership, insurance, payment, or other documents. Confirm directly with the official local source before traveling to the lot.

Can I find a towed vehicle by license plate?

Sometimes. Some local towing or impound systems accept a license plate, VIN, or other vehicle details. Availability depends on the city, county, agency, or towing system.

How much does it cost to retrieve a towed car?

There is no single nationwide price. Costs can include the tow itself, storage, administrative charges, and other authorized fees. Storage fees may continue to increase while the vehicle remains impounded.
